Probability Experiments
A report exploring probability through simulations of Bayes’ theorem and the binomial probability distribution.
Contents
Bayes’ Theorem and Inverse Probability
This section shows how test sensitivity, test specificity, and the base rate affect the chance that a condition is present after a positive test result.

The report compares five combinations of these values and summarizes the resulting probabilities.
Binomial Probability Distribution
This section explores how the number of trials and the chance of success affect the distribution of outcomes.

It presents six examples, including distributions that are nearly symmetric, skewed toward lower or higher outcomes, concentrated near zero, or limited to one possible outcome.
Key Takeaways
- The meaning of a positive test result depends on the base rate and test specificity as well as test sensitivity.
- The shape of a binomial distribution changes with the number of trials and the chance of success.
- When success and failure are about equally likely, the distribution can be nearly symmetric.
- When success is very unlikely or nearly certain, outcomes cluster toward one end of the range.
