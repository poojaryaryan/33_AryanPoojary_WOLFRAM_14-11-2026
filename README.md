# Probability Experiments

An exploration of probability using simulations of **Bayes’ theorem** and the **binomial probability distribution**.

## Bayes’ Theorem and Inverse Probability

This section examines how **sensitivity**, **specificity**, and the **base rate** affect the chance that a condition is present after a positive test result.

The report compares five combinations of these values and summarizes the resulting probabilities.

## Binomial Probability Distribution

This section explores how the number of trials and the chance of success shape the distribution of outcomes.

Six examples illustrate distributions that are nearly symmetric, skewed toward lower or higher outcomes, concentrated near zero, or limited to a single possible outcome.

## Key Takeaways

- The meaning of a positive test result depends on sensitivity, specificity, and the base rate.
- The number of trials and the chance of success affect the shape of a binomial distribution.
- When success and failure are about equally likely, the distribution can be nearly symmetric.
- When success is very unlikely or nearly certain, outcomes cluster toward one end of the range.
